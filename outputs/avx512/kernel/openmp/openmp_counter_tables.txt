Table 3: Serial against 32-thread FP32, N = 4.19 x 10^6. Threading adds
instructions rather than removing them: thread setup, barriers and parallel-loop
bookkeeping are all retired instructions the serial build never executes. The
FP-arithmetic counters exclude that overhead, which is why they stay exact while
the total does not. task-clock is CPU-time summed over threads, so it rises with
the thread count while elapsed time falls; perf's derived "GHz" figure is
therefore meaningless in threaded mode (it reported 0.886 GHz against a true
~2.7 GHz) but IPC stays valid, both terms being process-wide totals.

        Threads within 2% of median               29 / 32       29 / 32

Table 4: Per-thread breakdown at 32 threads, N = 6.71 x 10^7. The total divided
by 32 is 9 984.5 M against a median of 9 888.0 M, so the work is split evenly
between threads and not duplicated; the mean sits just above the median because
of three outliers. Those outliers are elevated in both instructions and cycles
while their IPC falls (1.49-1.60 against 1.64 for the rest), which is the
signature of spin-waiting at a barrier: an idle thread still retires
instructions while it spins. A larger N is used here than in Tables 1-3
because perf's --per-thread mode can only attach to a running process, and at
the smaller N the run finished before the attach completed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reproducing
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
From src/AVX-512/ on mimic, after sourcing /opt/intel/oneapi/setvars.sh:

    make kbench_perf      KERNEL=fp32                        # serial, 3 passes
    make kbench_perf_omp  KERNEL=fp32 OMPTHREADS=32         # threaded, 3 passes
    make kbench_perf_omp_percpu KERNEL=fp32 OMPTHREADS=32 \
         NPATHS=67108864 PERTHREAD_REPS=60                   # per-thread

Both OpenMP targets pin threads with OMP_PLACES=cores and OMP_PROC_BIND=close so
the counts are not disturbed by thread migration. perf stat -A was deliberately
not used: it reports per-CPU rather than per-thread and requires system-wide
mode, which would also count other users' work on this shared node.

Output files:
    basket_l0_perf_omp32_{fp32,fp16}.txt        Tables 2 and 3
    basket_l0_perthread_omp32_{fp32,fp16}.txt   Table 4
    ../basket/basket_l0_perf_{fp32,fp16}.txt    Table 1 and the serial columns
